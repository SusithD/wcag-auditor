Footbridge over Cutler Creek reopens after fourteen months

By the Courier news desk · Updated at 09:15

Walkers, cyclists and one dog crossed Cutler Creek on Friday morning as the footbridge reopened to the public for the first time since August last year. The bridge was closed after a normal inspection found rot in two of the main timbers, and the repair took far longer than the town council first promised.

Councillor Mara Penhallow, who chairs the harbour committee, cut a ribbon made of fishing line and told the crowd that the bridge was “stronger, wider and much less slippery.” The ramp on the east side has been rebuilt at a shallower angle, so wheelchair users and parents with pushchairs no longer need to detour half a mile by road.

Shopkeepers on Quay Street, who say footfall fell by nearly a fifth while the crossing was shut, welcomed the news. “We waited for this every day,” said baker Tobias Reed. The bridge also links the school with the estuary path, and teachers expect fewer late arrivals now that the short route is back.

The final bill came to £184,000, funded by a mix of council reserves and a coastal heritage grant. A full breakdown will be published at the next open council meeting. Residents can also read the safety inspection summary through the letters desk or ask a question by post.
